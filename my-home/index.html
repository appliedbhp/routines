    4 · PLAN YOUR SUPPORT

    When my child gets distracted…

    Agree on what help feels useful before the routine starts. Losing their place is a cue to offer support.

    If the first cue does not help

    Pause and give time to respond. Avoid piling on reminders. Check whether the step is unclear, too big, or uncomfortable. Make it smaller, offer help starting, or offer a needed break with a manageable return step.

    Ask what reminders feel helpful. Pictures, gestures, objects, and words are all options. Do not require eye contact or touch. Notice their return in a way they like: “You found your next step.”

    As the routine gets easier, try a smaller cue while keeping help available. Independence does not mean removing needed support.
